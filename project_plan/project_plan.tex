\section{Problem}

The optimizer decides which of many equal-loss solutions training returns. In linear models each
first-order method converges to the max-margin solution of its own norm: $\ell_2$ for gradient descent
\citep{soudry2018separable}, $\ell_\infty$ for Adam \citep{zhang2024adam} and the spectral norm for Muon
\citep{fan2025spectral}. In deep networks we only see the end point: adaptive methods were found to
generalize worse \citep{wilson2017marginal}, then to learn richer features \citep{vasudeva2025rich}, and
on language models most optimizers reach similar loss \citep{zhao2025deconstructing}. We ask
\emph{when} the difference appears. From the same initialization and the same data order, do SGD,
AdamW and Muon first follow the same path in function space and only later separate? If so, where is
the split, what happens in the optimization at that moment, and is the final difference already
decided there?

In vision, \citet{jastrzebski2020breakeven} show SGD runs with different learning rates separating in
prediction space, and tie the early dynamics to a \emph{break-even point}, the first time training
meets a stability boundary along the sharpest direction. We test whether the same picture holds across optimizers, on a small language
model, and whether the split coincides with an edge-of-stability event
\citep{cohen2021eos,cohen2022adaptive,andreyev2024eoss}.

\section{What we plan to achieve}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Divergence curves.} The function-space distance between optimizers over training, compared
both at equal steps and at equal training loss, so that ``faster'' is not confused with ``different''.
\item \textbf{A located split point} $t^\ast$ for each pair of optimizers, with uncertainty, defined
against the seed-to-seed spread of a single optimizer.
\item \textbf{What coincides with $t^\ast$}: sharpness reaching its stability threshold, the end of
warmup, or a loss level.
\item \textbf{A causal check by swapping}: swapping optimizer before $t^\ast$ should end at the new
optimizer's function, and swapping after $t^\ast$ should stay near the old one.
\item \textbf{Moonshot}: which layers carry the sharp direction at the edge of stability, by optimizer.
\end{enumerate}

\section{How we plan to achieve it}

\paragraph{Setup.} nanoGPT \citep{karpathy2022nanogpt}, a small decoder-only transformer, on
character-level Shakespeare (minutes per run) and then TinyStories at about 10M and 30M parameters.
Optimizers: SGD with momentum, AdamW, and Muon for the hidden matrices with AdamW on embeddings and
head, as in \citet{jordan2024muon}. Each optimizer gets a short learning-rate sweep, and we keep the
best rate. All runs share the initialization and the batch order. A small CNN on CIFAR-10 reproduces
the vision result as a check of the pipeline.

\paragraph{Function distance.} On a fixed held-out set of contexts we log, every few hundred steps,
the next-token distributions of each run. The distance between two runs is the mean Jensen--Shannon
divergence between their predictions, plus top-1 agreement. The noise floor is the distance between
two runs of the \emph{same} optimizer with different data order. We align runs in two ways: by step,
and by training loss (each checkpoint matched to the other run's checkpoint with the same loss).

\paragraph{Locating the split.} $t^\ast$ is the first point where the loss-matched distance between
two optimizers exceeds the noise floor by a fixed margin over several consecutive checkpoints, with a
bootstrap over seeds for its uncertainty. As a second, basin-level test we measure linear mode
connectivity \citep{frankle2020lmc}: the loss barrier between two runs forked from a common checkpoint
with different optimizers, as a function of the fork time.

\paragraph{What happens at the split.} Along each run we track the top Hessian eigenvalue by power
iteration on a fixed batch: $\lambda_{\max}$ for SGD, the preconditioned version for AdamW
\citep{cohen2022adaptive}, and batch sharpness \citep{andreyev2024eoss}. We compare $t^\ast$ with the
step at which each reaches its threshold, and with the end of warmup.

\paragraph{Swap.} We fork optimizer $A$ at times before, at and after $t^\ast$ and continue with $B$.
$B$'s state is accumulated in shadow during $A$'s run, so $B$ starts warm. The control is $A$
continued. We report where each fork lands between pure $A$ and pure $B$, by the distance above and by a few
function metrics: input-Jacobian norm, token-substitution sensitivity \citep{hahn2021sensitivity},
and the $\ell_2$, $\ell_\infty$ and spectral weight norms each optimizer is predicted to keep small.

\paragraph{Moonshot: the anatomy of the edge of stability.}
At each logged step we take the top Hessian eigenvector $v$ and split it by parameter block:
embeddings, the query-key and value-output matrices and the MLP of each layer, the norms and the head.
For block $b$ we record its share of the direction, $\|v_b\|^2$, and its share of the sharpness,
$v_b^\top (Hv)_b$, whose sum over blocks is $\lambda_{\max}$. The questions: which components carry
the sharpness when training reaches the edge of stability, whether this moves across layers during
training, and whether it differs by optimizer, given that transformer Hessians differ strongly across
blocks \citep{zhang2024hessian}. If the block that dominates changes near $t^\ast$, the two halves of
the project meet.
