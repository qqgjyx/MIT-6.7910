\subsection{Rules without learning}
\label{sec:noise}

Each rule updates one block matrix of each shape for 10{,}000 steps on synthetic gradients: noise, or a rank-4 direction plus noise. Nothing is learned (\cref{fig:noise}).

\textbf{Update cosine follows the rule.} On noise, consecutive updates have cosine 0.90 under AdamW and 0.66 to 0.76 under Muon, close to the real runs (0.89 and 0.74).

\textbf{The spectra do not.} On noise, neither rule moves the stable rank of $W$. With the rank-4 signal both fall to about its rank, AdamW's within a few hundred steps and Muon's by the end (step 1000: 3.7 against 9.9).

\subsection{Ghosts on real gradients}
\label{sec:ghost}

Each run carries a \emph{ghost}: a copy of the initial weights updated by the other optimizer's rule on the run's own gradients. The ghost never feeds back into the run. With the two runs this makes a $2\times2$ design, rule $\times$ gradient stream (\cref{tab:ghost}, \cref{fig:ghost}).

\textbf{The ordering follows the rule.} Muon's rule on AdamW's gradients ends at stable rank 87, against 40 for the AdamW run and 75 for the Muon run. AdamW's rule on Muon's gradients ends at 13. $\sigma_1/\sigma_{\mathrm{median}}$ and update cosine go the same way. Both ghosts go further than the run with their rule (87 against 75, 13 against 40), so learning pulls the two optimizers together.

\textbf{The spectral norm does not.} On AdamW's gradients Muon's rule gives 10.9 and AdamW's 5.9; on Muon's gradients, 7.5 and 7.8. Which rule is larger depends on the gradient stream.

\textbf{Not shown.} A ghost has no loss or Hessian, so it says nothing about $\lambda_{\max}$, MLP sparsity or the loss.

\subsection{What the speed needs}
\label{sec:ablate}

Three ablations: AdamW without weight decay, Muon with weight decay 0.02, and Muon without orthogonalization (\cref{tab:ablate}).

\textbf{Weight decay is not the reason.} Muon has none and is already faster; removing it from AdamW costs a little (1.346 to 1.353 against 1.339 to 1.345) and adding it to Muon helps a little (1.297 to 1.304 against 1.308 to 1.310).

\textbf{Without orthogonalization Muon trains worse than AdamW.} At learning rate 0.01 the final loss is 3.729, 2.943 and 1.389, and no rate tried (three, on one data order) reaches AdamW's 1.339. So orthogonalization is necessary for Muon's speed.
