\paragraph{Question.} From the same initialization and the same sequence of mini-batches, do SGD with momentum,
AdamW and Muon first follow the same path in function space and only later separate? If so, where is the split,
what coincides with it (an edge-of-stability event \citep{cohen2021eos,cohen2022adaptive}, the end of warmup, a
loss level), and is the final function already decided there?

\paragraph{What we have done.} We trained nanoGPT \citep{karpathy2022nanogpt} on character-level
Shakespeare (10.8M parameters) and TinyStories-10M and -30M (14M and 30M parameters) with SGD, AdamW and Muon
\citep{jordan2024muon}, at learning rates from a sweep, from a shared initialization and with five data orders
per optimizer. Along training we logged the next-token probability distributions on fixed held-out contexts,
and the top eigenvalue of the Hessian and, for AdamW, of the preconditioned Hessian \citep{cohen2022adaptive},
both computed with the Lanczos method \citeyearpar{lanczos1950}. We swapped optimizers at nine fork steps with warm
optimizer states, measured linear mode connectivity, and replicated the CIFAR-10 break-even analysis
\citep{jastrzebski2020breakeven}. In total 392 runs, about 103 GPU-hours.

\paragraph{Method.} The distance between two runs is the mean Jensen--Shannon divergence of their next-token
predictions. We compare runs at equal \emph{training loss}, so that one optimizer being faster is not counted
as a difference, and divide by a noise floor: the distance between two runs of the same optimizer that differ
only in data order. A split is a stretch of loss levels where this ratio exceeds 1.5 for three consecutive
levels (a hand-set margin; 1.25, 2 and another metric give the same conclusions); it is persistent
if it lasts to the end of training, with a bootstrap over data orders for its location.\looseness=-1

\paragraph{Result 1: at equal loss, no split after warmup (\cref{fig:overview}).} After warmup, the
distance between optimizers at equal loss stays at about the data-order floor to the end of training, in all
three settings and for every pair except Muon--SGD on Shakespeare, where SGD barely trains; that split is lost
with another metric or a stricter threshold. The only stretches above the floor are brief and within warmup,
where the floor is near zero because the runs share their initialization. Muon is faster (\cref{fig:div}, right; AdamW needs 1.35 and
1.22 times as many steps as Muon to reach its final validation loss on TinyStories-10M and -30M), yet at equal
loss it lands on the same function as AdamW up to data-order noise: at the end of TinyStories-30M, replacing AdamW
by Muon changes the predictions no more than changing the data order. After warmup (\cref{fig:div}, left), AdamW
and Muon with the same data order are nearest neighbours in the embedding, with Muon at lower loss at each step,
yet only 5--20\% closer in $\sqrt{\mathrm{JS}}$ than two data orders of one optimizer, from their shared recent
batches; at equal loss this vanishes. So the answer to the title is no: after warmup we find no
split, early or late; the optimizers' functions are as close as the data order lets them be.

\paragraph{Result 2: the last optimizer decides the function (\cref{fig:swap}).} When we switch optimizer at
step $t$, the fork ends within the floor of the new optimizer whenever that optimizer is fast enough to get
there in the remaining steps: forks from SGD to AdamW or Muon land on the new optimizer even after 4000 of
10{,}000 steps, while forks into the slower SGD end closer to their parent from step 1500 on (step 5000 on
TinyStories-30M). The endpoint is thus not decided early. Weight space keeps a mark that function space does
not: after a change of optimizer, a fork needs a later fork step to stay linearly connected to its parent
\citep{frankle2020lmc} than after a change of data order.

\paragraph{Result 3 (moonshot): the sharp direction at the edge of stability.} Restricted to the steps at each
optimizer's threshold and averaged over the five data orders, the top eigenvector of the operator that sets the
threshold lies at the input end: the embeddings and first three blocks carry 75--90\% of it in all settings.
SGD's lies mostly in the first block; AdamW's, of $P^{-1/2}HP^{-1/2}$, in the embeddings on TinyStories (about
60\%), where rare tokens make $P^{-1}$ largest; Muon's, without a threshold, in its first three blocks (on
Shakespeare in the embeddings, left to its auxiliary AdamW). Except in the embeddings, 66--83\% of the
eigenvalue comes from curvature \emph{between} blocks. The direction changes block long after warmup,
when the optimizers are already at the floor, so it does not mark a split.

\paragraph{Result 4: a model of the results (proof sketch).} Let $\mathcal F_s$ be the predictors that see only
the last $s$ tokens, with best element $q_s$. As $\sqrt{\mathrm{JS}}$ is a metric, JS (bits) $\le$ total
variation, and by Pinsker, two predictors in $\mathcal F_s$ with excess losses $\Delta L_A,\Delta L_B$ over $q_s$
have $\mathbb E\,\mathrm{JS}\le\sqrt2(\sqrt{\Delta L_A}+\sqrt{\Delta L_B})$, whatever the optimizer. So at
equal loss the bound forces the optimizers together near the bigram optimum and below, but not at higher loss,
where their routes can differ, as in the warmup stretches (SGD passes through the unigram model, Muon skips
it), nor in a class without a best element, as in the linear control below. The break-even point depends on how
the update scales with the gradient: $(2+2\mu)/\eta$ for SGD; for AdamW the threshold applies to $P^{-1}H$, so the raw sharpness there
scales as the gradient over $\eta$, as we find (log-log slope 1.15--1.6 on TinyStories); Muon's update is
invariant to the gradient's scale and on a quadratic never diverges, so it has no break-even point on its
matrices. Stability caps curvature but does not choose between functions of equal loss.\looseness=-1

\paragraph{Pipeline check and controls.} On CIFAR-10 the break-even effects replicate: the sharpness levels off
at or below $2/\eta$ and lower for larger rates, and the gradient covariance is better conditioned; but at equal
loss two learning rates differ by at most about twice the floor. Controls: a second initialization
reproduces the results, and on a linear model on separable data, where gradient descent and Adam have different
max-margin limits \citep{soudry2018separable,zhang2024adam}, the same test does find a persistent split.
