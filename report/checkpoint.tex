\paragraph{Summary.} We train one transformer from one initialization on one sequence of mini-batches with AdamW
and with Muon \citep{jordan2024muon}. (1)~Muon is ahead at every step, but at equal training loss its predictions are
as close to AdamW's as two data orders of one optimizer; the endpoints' loss barrier exceeds the data-order ones on one
of three datasets. (2)~What differs along the way, so far: how each optimizer sits at its stability threshold, how its
steps align with the population gradient, how far and how evenly it moves the weights. (3)~Swapping the two
mid-training leaves the function within the floor of both, while the weights keep part of the parent's geometry.

\paragraph{Setup.} The main setting is TinyStories \citep{eldan2023tinystories} with a 14M-parameter nanoGPT
\citep{karpathy2022nanogpt} (6 layers, width 384, 4096-token BPE, 10{,}000 steps of $128\times256$ tokens, warmup
over 200 steps, cosine decay to 10\%); a 30M model on TinyStories and a character-level model on Shakespeare are
checks (\cref{app:hparams}). Muon orthogonalizes the update of each matrix in the transformer blocks and leaves
embeddings, head and gains to an auxiliary AdamW; both optimizers use the best rate of a five-rate sweep. All runs
share one initialization; a data order fixes the mini-batch at every step, and each optimizer runs with five. We
store the next-token distributions on 2{,}048 fixed held-out positions; the distance between two runs is the mean
Jensen--Shannon divergence (JS, bits) of these predictions. We compare runs at equal \emph{training loss} (on 64
fixed training sequences), so that speed does not count as a difference, and against a \emph{floor}: the distance
between two data orders of one optimizer (the larger of the two means). So far 130 runs and 34 GPU-hours, with 27
more running (\cref{app:compute}).

\paragraph{Step 1: how the trajectories differ (\cref{fig:div,fig:equal}).} After warmup, AdamW and Muon on the same
data order stay close: in the embedding each such pair are nearest neighbours, and at equal steps their distance
stays below that between two data orders of one optimizer. Muon is ahead: its validation loss is lower at every
step (2.46 against 2.73 at step 250, 1.75 against 1.92 at step 1000; means over five data orders), and it reaches
AdamW's final value, 1.344, by step 7500 of 10{,}000. At equal training loss (\cref{fig:equal}, left), the distance
between them follows the data-order floors after warmup: below loss 4.85 it is 0.95--1.38 times the floor (median
1.02), and at the lowest loss all runs reach, 0.99 times (TinyStories-30M and Shakespeare: 1.02). In function space
the two optimizers reach the same endpoint, up to the noise of the data order.

In weight space the endpoints differ. On the straight line between two final weights, the training loss rises above
the line between its end values by a \emph{barrier} (maximum over 11 points; \citealp{frankle2020lmc}). Between AdamW
and Muon on one data order it is 3.5--4.1 nats on TinyStories-10M (mean 3.79), against 1.20--1.88 (mean 1.54) between
two data orders of AdamW and 1.58--1.77 (mean 1.67) of Muon (\cref{fig:equal}, right). Only there is the change of
optimizer clearly the larger one: on TinyStories-30M the AdamW--Muon barrier (mean 4.64) is above Muon's data-order
barriers (mean 1.79) but below AdamW's (mean 5.05, range 3.3--6.8), and on Shakespeare (2.42) above AdamW's (0.14) but
below Muon's (4.34). No two endpoints are linearly connected.

\paragraph{Step 2: what differs along the way (\cref{tab:step2}).} If the functions agree at equal loss, the optimizers
differ in how they get there. To see what is specific to each geometry we add, in this step only, SGD with heavy-ball
momentum 0.9 at rate 0.06 (the only rate of 0.03--0.12 that never blew up on three data orders). Each optimizer sits at
its own stability threshold very differently: AdamW's preconditioned sharpness for most of training, SGD's top
Hessian eigenvalue only around the end of warmup, and Muon's spectral-norm sharpness at most once, in warmup. Along
the step each one takes, the second-order loss change on fresh data nearly cancels the first-order one for all three
($\bar R\approx1$); the adaptive steps are several times less aligned with the population gradient ($\rho$) and
sharper on their own batch ($\kappa$). At the end, Muon has moved the weights twice as far as AdamW and ten times as
far as SGD, with the most even singular values. To rank such quantities as the functions are ranked, at equal
training loss and against their spread over data orders, we are measuring about 30 whole-model metrics on nine new
runs (three optimizers, three data orders) with checkpoints at consecutive steps.

\paragraph{Step 3: swaps (\cref{fig:swap}).} We switch between AdamW and Muon at nine fork steps from 50 to 4000,
with a warm optimizer state and the parent's batches (\cref{app:forks}). Every fork ends
within the data-order floor of the new optimizer's pure run (0.86--0.95 floors) and, increasingly with the fork step,
of the parent's (0.92--0.98 at step 50, 0.20--0.21 at step 4000): as the pure runs end within each other's floor,
the function cannot tell which optimizer decided it. The weights can. A fork stays linearly connected to its parent
(barrier below 0.05 nats) only from a later fork step than a change of data order: from AdamW at step 2500 (0.03)
against 1000 (0.035), from Muon not even at step 4000 (0.055) against 1000 (0.046). No fork ends in the basin of the
new optimizer's pure run (barriers 3.7--4.3 nats into Muon and 2.9--6.2 into AdamW, against data-order barriers of
1.6--1.8 and 1.2--1.9). Switching at step 4000 moves the stable rank only 0.27 (into Muon) and 0.46 (into AdamW) of the
way to the new optimizer's value, and the distance from the initialization 0.49 and 0.70.
