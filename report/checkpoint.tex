\title{Together First, Apart Later? When Optimizers Start to Learn Different Functions\\[1pt]
\normalfont Initial checkpoint, MIT 6.7910, Fall 2026}
\author{Juntang Wang and V\'ictor Conchello}
\date{November 1, 2026}

\vspace{-0.8em}

\paragraph{Summary.} We train one transformer from one initialization on one sequence of mini-batches with AdamW
and with Muon \citep{jordan2024muon}. (1)~At equal training loss their predictions are as close as those of two data
orders of one optimizer; the loss barrier between their endpoints exceeds the data-order ones on one of three
datasets. (2)~Along the way they differ in geometry, not in predictions: Muon's updates and weights have flat
spectra, AdamW's a few dominant directions, fewer active units and a flatter loss. (3)~Swapping the two mid-training
leaves the function within the floor of both, while the weights keep part of the parent's geometry.

\paragraph{Setup.} The main setting is TinyStories \citep{eldan2023tinystories} with a 14M-parameter nanoGPT
\citep{karpathy2022nanogpt} (6 layers, width 384, 10{,}000 steps of $128\times256$ tokens, cosine decay); a 30M model
on TinyStories and a character-level model on Shakespeare are checks (\cref{app:hparams}). Muon orthogonalizes the
update of each block matrix and leaves embeddings, head and gains to an auxiliary AdamW; each optimizer uses the best
rate of a five-rate sweep. All runs share one initialization; a data order fixes the mini-batch at every step (five
per optimizer). The distance between two runs is the mean Jensen--Shannon divergence (JS, bits) of their next-token
distributions on 2{,}048 fixed held-out positions. We compare runs at equal \emph{training loss} (on 64 fixed
training sequences), so that speed does not count, and against a \emph{floor}: the distance between two data orders
of one optimizer (the larger of the two means). In all, 123 runs and 31 GPU-hours, with 18 more running (\cref{app:compute}).

\paragraph{Step 1: how the trajectories differ (\cref{fig:div,fig:equal}).} After warmup, AdamW and Muon on the same
data order stay close: each such pair are nearest neighbours in the embedding, and closer than two data orders of one
optimizer at equal steps. Muon is ahead (validation loss 1.75 against 1.92 at step 1000, means of five data orders; it
reaches AdamW's final 1.344 by step 7500). At equal training loss (\cref{fig:equal}, left) their distance follows the
data-order floors: below loss 4.85 it is 0.95--1.38 floors (median 1.02), and 0.99 at the lowest loss all runs reach
(TinyStories-30M and Shakespeare: 1.02). In function space both reach the same endpoint, up to the data-order noise.

In weight space they do not. The training loss on the segment between two final weights rises above the line between
its end values by a \emph{barrier} (maximum over 11 points; \citealp{frankle2020lmc}): 3.5--4.1 nats between AdamW
and Muon on TinyStories-10M, against 1.2--1.9 between two data orders of AdamW and 1.6--1.8 of Muon
(\cref{fig:equal}, right). Only there is the change of optimizer clearly the larger: on TinyStories-30M its mean (4.64)
lies between Muon's data-order mean (1.78) and AdamW's (5.05), and on Shakespeare (2.42) between AdamW's (0.14) and
Muon's (4.34).

\paragraph{Step 2: what differs along the way (\cref{fig:rank}).} To see what is specific to each geometry we add, in this step only, SGD with
heavy-ball momentum 0.9 at rate 0.06 (the only rate of 0.03--0.12 that never blew up on three data orders). With
three data orders per optimizer we measure 31 whole-model metrics at 34 checkpoints (weights, one-step updates,
Hessian, predictions, activations; \cref{app:metrics}) and score each pair of optimizers as the functions: their gap
at equal training loss on one data order over the gap between two data orders (values below at losses 2.96--1.81).
No prediction metric separates AdamW from Muon (scores 0.6--1.2), but their geometry does. Muon's updates have stable
rank 190--220 against 7--17 for AdamW, by construction (it orthogonalizes them); less trivially, its weights keep the
flatter spectra (stable rank 86--104 against 28--33, $\sigma_1/\sigma_{\rm median}$ 2.5--2.7 against 6--8; scores 147
and 105). AdamW concentrates the model: fewer active MLP units (12--28\% against 22--43\%; score 62), a larger
residual stream (RMS 1.3--4.7 against 0.66--3.4; 28), lower attention entropy (2.5--3.1 against 2.8--4.2 nats; 18) and
a flatter loss ($\operatorname{tr}H$ 1.4--3.8k against 2.8--7.9k; 23). Yet both move equally far from the
initialization (2.2$\|\theta_0\|$ at loss 1.81; score 3.4). SGD is the outlier there: it moves 3--4.5 times less, in
steps about 10 times smaller and 2--3 times better aligned with their batch gradient (cosine 0.45--0.48 against
0.15--0.24), on a far sharper loss ($\operatorname{tr}H$ 21--25k). The stability thresholds differ too: AdamW's
preconditioned sharpness is at least 0.9 of $38/\eta_t$ \citep{cohen2022adaptive} at 50--65\% of the measured steps,
SGD's $\lambda_{\max}$ of $3.8/\eta_t$ at 6--10\%, Muon's spectral-norm sharpness of its bound
\citep{islamov2026noneuclidean} once, at step 50.

\paragraph{Step 3: swaps (\cref{fig:swap}).} We switch between AdamW and Muon at nine fork steps from 50 to 4000,
with a warm optimizer state and the parent's batches (\cref{app:forks}). Every fork ends within the data-order floor
of the new optimizer's pure run (0.86--0.95 floors) and, increasingly with the fork step, of the parent's (0.92--0.98
at step 50, 0.20--0.21 at step 4000): the function cannot tell which optimizer decided it. The weights can. A fork
stays linearly connected to its parent (barrier below 0.05 nats) only from a later fork step than a change of data
order: from AdamW at step 2500 (0.03) against 1000 (0.035), from Muon not even at step 4000 (0.055) against 1000
(0.046), and none ends in the basin of the new optimizer's run (third panel). Switching at step 4000 moves the stable rank only 0.27
(into Muon) and 0.46 (into AdamW) of the way to the new optimizer's value, and the distance from the initialization
0.49 and 0.70.
