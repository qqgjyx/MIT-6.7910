\paragraph{Question.} From the same initialization and the same sequence of mini-batches, do SGD with momentum,
AdamW and Muon first follow the same path in function space and only later separate? If so, where is the split,
what coincides with it (an edge-of-stability event \citep{cohen2021eos,cohen2022adaptive}, the end of warmup, a
loss level), and is the final function already decided there?

\paragraph{What we have done.} We trained nanoGPT \citep{karpathy2022nanogpt} on character-level
Shakespeare (10.8M parameters) and TinyStories-10M and -30M (14M and 30M; \cref{app:hparams-fig1,app:hparams-other}) with SGD, AdamW and Muon
\citep{jordan2024muon}, at learning rates from a sweep, from a shared initialization and with five data orders
per optimizer. Along training we logged the next-token probability distributions on fixed held-out contexts
and, for the training loss on fixed training sequences, the top eigenvalue of the Hessian and, for AdamW, of the
preconditioned Hessian \citep{cohen2022adaptive}, both with the Lanczos method \citeyearpar{lanczos1950}. We
swapped optimizers at nine fork steps with warm states, measured linear mode connectivity, and replicated the CIFAR-10 break-even analysis
\citep{jastrzebski2020breakeven}. In total 404 runs, about 105 GPU-hours.

\paragraph{Method.} The distance between two runs is the mean Jensen--Shannon divergence of their next-token
predictions. We compare runs at equal \emph{training loss}, so that one optimizer being faster is not counted
as a difference, and divide by a noise floor: the distance between two runs of the same optimizer that differ
only in data order. A split is a stretch of loss levels where this ratio exceeds 1.5 for three consecutive
levels (a hand-set margin; 1.25, 2 and another metric give the same conclusions); it is persistent
if it lasts to the end of training, with a bootstrap over data orders for its location.\looseness=-1

\paragraph{Result 1: AdamW and Muon (\cref{fig:div}).} From the end of warmup, AdamW and Muon trained on the
same data order stay close: in the embedding (left) each pair are nearest neighbours, and their divergence
(middle) stays below that between two runs of the same optimizer with different data orders. Even so, Muon is at a much lower loss at the same step (2.50 vs 2.76
at step 250, 1.77 vs 1.95 at step 1000) and stays below AdamW to the end (right); AdamW needs 1.35 times as many
steps to reach its final validation loss. Towards the end of training all six runs gather in one cluster, as their
divergences shrink.

\paragraph{Result 2: equal loss, and SGD (\cref{fig:overview}).} After warmup, when their training loss is about
the same, two runs with different optimizers are about as far apart as two runs of one optimizer with different
data orders, with one exception: on TinyStories-10M, SGD is up to about 1.5 times as far from AdamW or Muon at
losses 4--2.5 (1.3 times on average), then within 1.1 times below loss 2.5. On TinyStories-30M every pair stays
within 1.1 times. The same holds for one optimizer at different learning rates ($\tfrac14$ to 4 times its rate,
TinyStories-10M). So we find no lasting split.

\paragraph{Result 3: the last optimizer decides the function (\cref{fig:swap}).} When we switch optimizer at
step $t$, the fork ends within the floor of the new optimizer whenever that optimizer is fast enough to get
there in the remaining steps: forks from SGD to AdamW or Muon land on the new optimizer even after 4000 of
10{,}000 steps, while forks into the slower SGD end closer to their parent from step 1500 on (step 5000 on
TinyStories-30M). The endpoint is thus not decided early. Weight space keeps a mark that function space does
not: after a change of optimizer, a fork needs a later fork step to stay linearly connected to its parent
\citep{frankle2020lmc} than after a change of data order.

\paragraph{Result 4 (moonshot): the sharp direction at the edge of stability.} At each optimizer's
threshold, the top eigenvector of the operator that sets it lies at the input end: SGD's in blocks 1--2 (66\%), AdamW's, of $P^{-1/2}HP^{-1/2}$, in the embeddings (68\%), where rare tokens make $P^{-1}$ largest,
and Muon's, which has no threshold on $H$ \citep{islamov2026noneuclidean}, in blocks 1--3 (84\%). Except in
AdamW's embeddings, most of the eigenvalue comes from curvature \emph{between} layers (66\% for SGD, 71\% for
Muon). The layer that holds the direction keeps changing long after the optimizers have re-met (AdamW's between
the embeddings and block 1 throughout training, Muon's from block 1 to 2 after step 6000), so it does not mark a
split.

\paragraph{Result 5: why the optimizers meet (proof sketch).} \textbf{Two models whose loss is close to the best
achievable loss must make nearly the same predictions, whichever optimizer trained them.} This follows from
Pinsker's inequality: for predictors that see only the last $s$ tokens, with best element $q_s$, two predictors
with excess losses $\Delta L_A,\Delta L_B$ over $q_s$ have $\mathbb E\,\mathrm{JS}\le\sqrt2(\sqrt{\Delta
L_A}+\sqrt{\Delta L_B})$, since $\sqrt{\mathrm{JS}}$ is a metric and JS (bits) is at most the total variation. The
bound says nothing at high loss, or when no best predictor exists: on separable data the loss can fall forever,
gradient descent and Adam reach different max-margin solutions \citep{soudry2018separable,zhang2024adam}, and our
linear control below does split. \textbf{The stability thresholds limit how sharp the loss gets, not which
function of a given loss is reached.} SGD's threshold is $(2+2\mu)/\eta$ on $H$; AdamW's applies to $P^{-1}H$
\citep{cohen2022adaptive}; Muon's, whose update ignores the gradient's scale, bounds a spectral-norm sharpness by
$2\|\nabla L\|_*/\eta$ \citep{islamov2026noneuclidean}. We find all three top eigenvalues falling about as
$1/\eta$ (slopes $-0.96$, $-0.86$, $-0.84$ on TinyStories-10M).

\paragraph{Pipeline check and controls.} On CIFAR-10 the break-even effects replicate: the sharpness levels off
at or below $2/\eta$ and lower for larger rates, and the gradient covariance is better conditioned; but at equal
loss two learning rates differ by at most about twice the floor. Controls: a second initialization
reproduces the results, and on a linear model on separable data, where gradient descent and Adam have different
max-margin limits \citep{soudry2018separable,zhang2024adam}, the same test does find a persistent split.
