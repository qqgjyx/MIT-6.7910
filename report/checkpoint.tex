\title{Similar Predictions, Different Weights:\\How AdamW and Muon Train a Small Language Model\\[1pt]
\normalfont Initial checkpoint, MIT 6.7910, Fall 2026}
\author{Juntang Wang and V\'ictor Conchello}
\date{November 1, 2026}

\maketitle
\vspace{-0.8em}

\paragraph{Summary.} We train a small language model with AdamW and with Muon, from one initialization and on the
same mini-batches. (1)~Muon trains faster and on a different path. At equal training
loss, their predictions differ about as much as two runs on different data orders; their final weights differ
more. (2)~Muon's weight and update matrices have more evenly sized singular values
than AdamW's, beyond data-order variation; no prediction statistic differs. (3)~A run switched to the other optimizer
mid-training ends up predicting no further from either optimizer's runs than two data orders do. Its updates
take the new optimizer's form; its weights and activations keep part of the old one's.

\paragraph{Setup.} \emph{Model.} We train a 14M-parameter nanoGPT \citep{karpathy2022nanogpt} on TinyStories
\citep{eldan2023tinystories} for 10{,}000 steps of 128 sequences of 256 tokens, with a cosine learning-rate decay
(\cref{app:hparams}).

\emph{Optimizers.} Muon \citep{jordan2024muon} orthogonalizes the update of each weight matrix in the transformer blocks: it sets the
update's singular values to about 1. AdamW trains Muon's other parameters. Each optimizer uses the best of five
learning rates. All runs start from the same weights. Each optimizer runs on the same five \emph{data orders}.

\paragraph{Step 1: how the trajectories differ (\cref{fig:div}).} \emph{Comparing runs.} The distance between two runs is the mean
Jensen--Shannon divergence (JS, in bits) of their next-token predictions on held-out text. We compare runs at equal
training loss, so that speed does not count as a difference. The \emph{floor} is the mean distance between two data
orders of one optimizer. A distance of one floor means ``as different as two
data orders''.

\emph{Faster, and on a different path.} After warmup, AdamW and Muon on the same data order stay close (a), and
Muon learns faster: it reaches AdamW's final validation loss, 1.344, by step 7500 (d). But it is not only faster. The
two share the initialization and the batches, so if Muon just moved faster along AdamW's path, their distance at
equal loss would be far below the floor. Instead it sits at the floor (c; median 1.02$\times$), while at
equal step it is below it (b; at step 1000, 0.102 against 0.131 at equal loss). Switching the optimizer changes the
path about as much as a new data order.

\emph{Final weights.} Their final weights are further apart than two data orders'. The \emph{loss barrier} between two weight vectors is
how far the training loss rises, on the straight path between them, above the line joining its end values
\citep{frankle2020lmc}. It is 3.5--4.1 nats between AdamW and Muon, against 1.2--1.9 between two data orders.

\paragraph{Step 2: what differs along the way.} We measure 30 properties of each model at 34 points of training, in
five groups: weights $W$, one-step updates $\Delta W$, loss curvature (Hessian $H$), internal activations and
predictions (\cref{app:metrics}). SGD with momentum, our reference, differs from both optimizers in most of them
(\cref{tab:app-sgd}).

We compare AdamW and Muon by the ratio of their values of each metric at training loss 1.81, with three data orders
each (\cref{fig:rank} for selected metrics, \cref{tab:app-am} for all). If their values range over
$[a_{\min},a_{\max}]$ and $[b_{\min},b_{\max}]$ across data orders, the ratio lies in
$[a_{\min}/b_{\max},\,a_{\max}/b_{\min}]$. We write it as midpoint $\pm$ half-width, the \emph{margin}. If the interval
excludes 1, the optimizers differ by more than data orders do.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Weights $W$.} Muon's weight matrices use their directions more evenly. Their stable rank
$\|W\|_F^2/\|W\|_2^2$ is 86 against AdamW's 33 ($2.61\pm0.04$ times), and their effective rank 325 against 272. Their
spectral norm (2.9 against 5.4) and largest-to-median singular-value ratio $\sigma_1/\sigma_{\rm median}$ (2.7 against
8.0) are lower. Both move equally far from the initialization (ratio $1.01\pm0.02$): they differ in the shape of
their weights, not in how far they travel.
\item \emph{One-step updates $\Delta W$.} Consecutive Muon steps are less alike (cosine 0.74 against 0.88). Their stable rank is much
higher (220 against 17; \cref{tab:app-am}), but by construction: Muon orthogonalizes every update.
\item \emph{Loss curvature (Hessian $H$).} Muon is about twice as sharp: the top Hessian eigenvalue $\lambda_{\max}$ is 14 against 6.2,
the trace $\operatorname{tr}H$ 2770 against 1400. But the tuned learning rates differ, and $\lambda_{\max}$ scales
roughly as one over the learning rate (log-log slopes $-0.86$ and $-0.84$ across a sweep). So part of this gap
reflects the rates, not the optimizer.
\item \emph{Internal activations.} AdamW's MLP is sparser: 12\% of its units are active, against 22\% for Muon.
\item \emph{Predictions.} No prediction metric separates the two: every AdamW/Muon interval contains 1 (\cref{tab:app-am}).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Step 3: swaps (\cref{fig:swap}).} A \emph{swap} switches a run of one optimizer (the \emph{parent}, on
data order 0) to the other optimizer at a \emph{fork step}, then trains it to step 10{,}000 on the parent's batches
(\cref{app:forks}). An optimizer's \emph{own run} is its run from the start.

We swap at nine fork steps from 50 to 4000, in both directions. In its predictions, every swapped run ends as similar to both its parent and the new optimizer's
own run as two data orders are: 0.86--0.95 floors from the own run, and 0.92--0.98 floors from the parent at fork
step 50 (0.20--0.21 at 4000).

Its weights are not. It is \emph{linearly connected} to its parent (barrier below 0.05) only from fork step 2500 when
leaving AdamW, and never when leaving Muon. Keeping the optimizer and switching only the data order gives this from
fork step 1000. The swapped run also stays further from the new optimizer's own run than that optimizer's data orders
are from each other (c).

In the final model (d), the updates take the new optimizer's form at every fork step: the stable rank of $\Delta W$
moves 0.99--1.04 of the way from the parent's value to the new optimizer's. The weights and activations keep part of
the parent's, more so the later the swap. At fork step 4000, the stable rank of $W$ moves 0.25 (AdamW to Muon) and
0.46 (Muon to AdamW) of the way, and the fraction of active MLP units 0.07 and 0.13.

\paragraph{Next steps.} (i)~Repeat Steps 1--3 on a larger model (TinyStories-30M) and on character-level Shakespeare.
(ii)~For each property that differs in Step 2: does it follow directly from Muon's orthogonalized updates, or does it
emerge during training? Does the sharpness gap come from the different tuned learning rates? (iii)~Do these
differences explain Step 1, Muon's faster training and different path? Is any of them a reason Muon does better?
