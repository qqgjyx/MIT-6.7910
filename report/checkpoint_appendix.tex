\section{Hyperparameters}
\label{app:hparams}

The values below are those of the configuration files and training code in the repository.

\subsection{Runs of Figure 1}
\label{app:hparams-fig1}

The 15 main TinyStories-10M runs (three optimizers, data orders d0--d4) share the following.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Model.} nanoGPT with pre-LayerNorm blocks: 6 layers, 6 heads, width 384, GELU MLPs of width
  1536, no biases, no dropout, untied output head, separate query, key and value projections; context 256
  tokens; 13.9M parameters (10.6M without the token and position embeddings and the head).
\item \textbf{Data.} TinyStories (GPT-4 version) with a 4096-token byte-level BPE trained on the training
  split. A batch is 128 windows of 256 tokens at offsets drawn from the training split by a generator seeded
  with (data order, step), the same for every optimizer.
\item \textbf{Length and schedule.} 10{,}000 steps, i.e.\ 328M tokens (about 0.6 epochs). Learning rate
  $\eta\,\varphi(t)$ at step $t$ for every parameter group: linear warmup $\varphi(t)=(t+1)/200$ for $t<200$,
  then cosine decay to $\varphi=0.1$ at step 10{,}000. No gradient clipping.
\item \textbf{Seeds and precision.} Initialization seed 0 for all runs; data orders 0--4. Training steps
  use bfloat16 autocast; predictions and Hessian measurements run without autocast, in float32 (TF32 matrix
  products allowed).
\item \textbf{Logged predictions.} Next-token distributions on 128 fixed validation sequences $\times$ 16
  evenly spaced positions (2{,}048 distributions, stored as float16 log-probabilities), and the loss on 64
  fixed training sequences (all positions), used to match runs by loss; at every 250th step and at 30
  log-spaced steps between 1 and 10{,}000.
\item \textbf{Hessian.} At every 500th step and 30 log-spaced steps: top eigenvalue by 20 Lanczos iterations
  on 32 fixed training sequences, for AdamW also that of $P^{-1/2}HP^{-1/2}$.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Runs of Figure 2}
\label{app:hparams-fig2}

The main runs of each setting (three optimizers, data orders d0--d4) are built as in
\cref{app:hparams-fig1}, except for the entries of \cref{tab:app-settings} and the following.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Shakespeare-char.} Character-level, nanoGPT's 90/10 split; 24.6M tokens (about 24 passes over
  the 1M training characters). AdamW uses $\beta=(0.9,0.99)$, as in nanoGPT; Muon's auxiliary AdamW keeps
  $(0.9,0.95)$. Predictions on all 256 positions of 64 validation sequences (16{,}384 distributions) at every
  50th step and 40 log-spaced steps; Hessian at every 50th step and 30 log-spaced steps.
\item \textbf{TinyStories-30M.} 655M tokens (about 1.2 epochs). Predictions at every 500th and Hessian at
  every 1000th step, each with 30 log-spaced steps.
\item \textbf{Shakespeare, long (memorization control).} AdamW and Muon only, data orders 0--4 (10 runs), as
  Shakespeare-char but 8{,}000 steps (131M tokens) with the cosine decay stretched to step 8{,}000;
  predictions at every 100th step and 40 log-spaced steps; no Hessian measurements.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Runs of Figure 3}
\label{app:hparams-fig3}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Parents.} The three d0 runs of \cref{app:hparams-fig1}. During the run of optimizer $A$, the
  other two optimizers are stepped on the same mini-batch gradients with learning rate 0 (shadow states):
  their momenta, second moments and step counts evolve as if they were training, while the parameters follow
  $A$ alone. At the fork steps 50, 100, 200, 400, 700, 1000, 1500, 2500 and 4000 the parent saves its
  parameters and the states of all three optimizers.
\item \textbf{Swaps.} The fork $A\to B$ at step $s$ loads the parameters at $s$ and $B$'s shadow state, uses
  $B$'s hyperparameters (\cref{tab:app-optim}), and continues from $s$ to step 10{,}000 on the same schedule
  (learning rate $\eta_B\,\varphi(s)$ at $s$) and the same batches (data order 0). Model, probes and logging
  grids are the parent's. All six ordered pairs at all nine steps: 54 forks.
\item \textbf{Controls.} $A$ continued with the same batches is the parent itself. $A$ continued from step
  $s$ with its own state but data order 1 is the data-order control: 27 forks, 81 in total.
\end{itemize}
